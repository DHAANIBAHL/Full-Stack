A college built around a coastline.

Founded in 1961, Spring Green has stayed deliberately small while the questions its students ask have only grown larger.

Our mission

Spring Green exists to give students time and room to think before they specialize. Every first-year takes courses across three divisions — sciences, humanities, and the arts — before choosing a major, so the choice is made with real footing rather than a guess made at seventeen.

We keep the college small on purpose. A faculty-to-student ratio under 1:14 means professors run their own labs and studios with undergraduates in them, not graduate assistants. Most students co-author a paper, exhibit a body of work, or publish original research before they finish their degree.

Port Ellery's harbor and salt marshes sit at the edge of campus, and a good portion of our teaching happens out there rather than in a lecture hall — a deliberate choice that shapes the college's curriculum, its research, and the students it tends to attract.

How we got here

1961

Spring Green opens with 84 students and a single building on Harbor Ridge, offering degrees in the natural sciences and letters.

1978

The Marsh Field Station opens, giving the biology and environmental science departments year-round access to the tidal estuary.

1994

The college adopts its open curriculum, removing distribution requirements in favor of a two-year exploratory period.

2011

Computer Science joins the sciences division, taught from the outset as a liberal art alongside its technical coursework.

2023

The Ellery Studio Building opens, consolidating the visual arts, print shop, and a public-facing gallery under one roof.
